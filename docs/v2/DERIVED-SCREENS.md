## Plate Frame — the appendix header on every v1 screen

**Route:** `components/student/plate-frame.tsx (new). Renders on /s/[slug]/waves, /waves/[debriefId], /patterns, /patterns/[pathId], /training, /home-base, /ask, and /s/[slug]?v1=1`  
**Sits beside:** ADDS a header band above v1 content. Restructures nothing: it is a sibling inserted by StudentShell, not an edit to any v1 page or component. pattern-trail.tsx (1,131 LOC), waves-list.tsx, patterns-home.tsx, training-client.tsx, back-home-view.tsx, ask-client.tsx and strategy-view.tsx are all untouched — zero diff.

THIS IS THE ANSWER TO THE CRUX (question 2). The load-bearing fact: v1's :root palette is byte-identical to v2's Abyss theme — --abyss #070f15 = --v2-bg abyss, --teal #2fd6c0 = --v2-accent abyss, --ink #f1eee6 = --v2-ink abyss, --glass-edge rgba(255,255,255,.10) = --v2-line abyss, --coral #ff6b5e = --v2-err abyss, --accent #e0b64f = --v2-gold abyss. The v1 screens are not un-designed; they are ALREADY IN A V2 THEME. They need zero colour work to be v2-legal. What they need is a frame that names them.

SO THE MODEL IS PLATES. In a real book, photographic and diagram plates are bound in on different stock and nobody reads that as unfinished. The Patterns trail is a map, the Strategy view is a diagram, the Waves list is a filmstrip — these ARE the figure content of this book. Calling them the appendix, printed on dark stock, is the correct reading of what they are, not a cop-out. The crossing works because it is prefigured on the paper page (see Plate Card), named on arrival, and one-way-labelled: v2 -> v1 is always 'APPENDIX', never 'Home', so the student's model is 'I stepped into the appendix', not 'the app changed'.

### RTL / i18n
The container takes dir from useLanguage().isRTL. Every offset is written as a logical property (inset-inline-start/end, padding-inline, margin-inline, border-inline-start), so the spread, the spine stub, the pill and the back arrow mirror for free.

DOES NOT MIRROR — three hard exceptions, and getting any of them wrong is the classic RTL login bug:
  1. THE PIN SLOT ROW. Digits are entered left-to-right in Hebrew too. The slot row carries direction:ltr explicitly and the input carries dir="ltr", even under RTL. Without this the first typed digit lands in the rightmost slot.
  2. THE COVER'S MONO SPINE TEXT ("VOL. III · YOUR COPY") and the row META line ("VOL. III · CLINIC 3 · LUANDA") — catalogue numbers, not prose. dir="ltr" on those nodes so the roman numeral and the middots do not reorder.
  3. The <mark> prefix highlight — it wraps the leading q.length characters, which under RTL is the rightmost run. That is correct and needs no special handling, but it must be computed on the string, not on visual position.

TYPE — the unresolved cost, and this is the cheapest screen on which to look at it. ### RTL / i18n
The breakpoint and both clamps are direction-agnostic; margin-inline:auto centres identically. Two more RTL-only token overrides: the +1px desktop --v2-fs bump is SUPPRESSED under [lang="he"] (Heebo's x-height is already larger than Literata's, so 18px Hebrew reads bigger than 18px Literata), and --v2-measure clamps to min(60ch, 42.5rem) rather than 66ch, because Hebrew at 60ch of Heebo occupies roughly the same 620–680px physical band as Latin at 66ch of Literata. THE MEASURE, SINCE TYPOGRAPHY IS THE PRODUCT.
--v2-measure: min(66ch, 42.5rem), resolved on a wrapper that itself carries font-size:var(--v2-fs) — otherwise ch resolves against the wrong size, which is the single easiest way to get this wrong. Desktop --v2-fs is calc(var(--v2-fs-step) + 1px), so the four stepper positions land at 16.5 / 18 / 19.5 / 21.5px. With Literata's digit advance at ~0.52em that gives 566 / 618 / 669 / 738px, and the 680px cap bites only at the top step, producing 66 / 66 / 66 / 63ch. Every stepper position sits inside the critic's 62-68ch band by construction, not by luck. Verify 0.52em against the real next/font webfont before freezing 680px; if ch measures differently, hold 66ch and move the px cap, never the reverse. Phone measure at 334px of text is 41 / 38 / 35 / 31ch, which is correct for a phone and unchanged. Line-height goes 1.78 to 1.82 at width, because a 66ch line needs more leading to track back than a 38ch one. In Hebrew the measure clamps to min(60ch, 42.5rem) and the +1px bump is suppressed — Heebo's larger x-height means matching the physical 620-680px band, not the character count.

TWO WIDTHS INSIDE THE PAGE, NOT ONE. .v2-col carries prose, block headings, chips, quotes, tinted cards, drills and the digest at the measure; .v2-plate carries the hero, film card, before/after pair, annotated still and end matter at 704px. Figures overhang the measure by ~43px per side at the default step and text and figures deliberately do not share a left edge — that overhang is the difference between a page and a web column. It is two custom properties and one class; deleting .v2-plate collapses everything to a single column with no other change, so it is a two-property risk, not an architecture.

THE PAGE ITSELF is two full-height 1px --v2-line rules on the reader's scroller band, with the header's border-bottom and the footer's border-top moved onto their clamped inner rows. Four rules, 800px wide, ground and page both --v2-bg. No shadow, no second surface colour, nothing invented. The TOC reuses --v2-page and the same rules on purpose — pressing ☰ must feel like flipping to the front of the same volume. The library deliberately does NOT get them and is wider (960px): the shelf is a room, the chapter is a page.

THE ONE THING THIS DERIVATION SURFACES THAT CHANGES A DECISION ALREADY ON THE BOARD. The page-count model is an open question in BUILD-PLAN-CRITIC, and the desktop layout settles half of it. pct is a ratio, so it is stable across widths — but the same chapter's scrollHeight at 1440px is roughly 2.4x smaller than at 390px. That means any page divisor derived from RENDERED HEIGHT reports a different page count on a phone and a laptop for the same chapter, and the resume position silently disagrees between devices. So: the page model must be WIDTH-INDEPENDENT — word count or a fixed divisor over block content, never rendered height. That is a direct consequence of shipping a desktop layout and it should go into the decision log before Step 4 starts. Relatedly, the digest stays collapsed by default at every width for the same reason: a width-dependent default expansion would make pct diverge across devices for no real gain.
